\begin{abstract}
Investigators need evidence that remains interpretable after collection, not just a large archive. We ask how collection policy, budget, and timing affect evidence preservation within the recoverable Collusion Wiki history. We implemented a conditional replay, a 65-proposition adjudicated benchmark, and retained-only core and context evaluation. The prespecified primary contrast compares changed-only periodic collection across 60 polling offsets with event-derived collection under a shared hypothetical feed. The experiment did not produce a policy-comparison result: none of 791 required rows completed, and the first exceeded a 7,200-second execution limit. A subsequent collection-only profile reduced five-day runtime from 154.869 to 21.199 seconds, but full-horizon feasibility remained unresolved. Thus the repository supports implementation and runtime findings, not an estimate of evidence loss or a preferred collection policy. The artifact separates source missingness from modeled non-preservation and makes completion, access assumptions, and retained context explicit prerequisites for that measurement.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}
Incident-response evidence must be acquired before it can be examined. Digital-forensics guidance already treats collection, integrity, and volatile data as operational concerns; log-management guidance covers storage and retention~\cite{nist86,nist92}. Our question is narrower: \emph{within the recoverable Collusion Wiki history, how do collection policy, budget, and timing affect human-validated evidence preservation?} We operationalize the evidence target through a team-assigned adjudication ledger, with the validation qualifications described below.

The released history provides saved page bodies and deletion records~\cite{export}. It does not provide a complete record of what every investigator could retrieve at every historical instant. We therefore implement a trace-grounded, live-only simulation under a shared hypothetical change-feed interface. A simulated collector's failure to retain a body would not establish that the website permanently lost it. The replay is the simulation; the released records are not treated as a synthetic agent population.

The intended contribution is a policy comparison in terms of distinct, interpretable propositions rather than revision counts or downloaded bytes. A repeated message can preserve the same proposition many times, while a retained fragment can lose the context needed to interpret it. These distinctions motivate the benchmark and evaluator. They do not supply an experimental result on their own.

\textbf{What was accomplished.} The repository contains a pinned data loader, a state-reconstruction engine, collection and storage implementations, an adjudicated benchmark, evaluator tests, and a prespecified comparison harness. The attempted experiment failed before completing its first row. This report presents the verified structural and runtime findings and states explicitly which scientific questions remain unanswered. Neither a null effect nor a preference between policies can be inferred from execution failure.

\section{Related Work and Contribution}
NIST SP 800-86 describes integrating forensic collection, examination, analysis, and reporting into incident response, including acquisition of volatile operating-system data~\cite{nist86}. NIST SP 800-92 addresses computer-security log management, including generation, protection, analysis, storage, and retention~\cite{nist92}. These establish that evidence acquisition and preservation are existing engineering problems. We do not claim to invent forensic logging, evidence preservation, integrity checking, or incident reconstruction.

The Collusion Wiki release is the empirical source for this project~\cite{export}. The investigators' reconstruction and publication make recoverable bodies and mutation records available. Our contribution is not rediscovering those records. It is an implementation for asking how a defined acquisition schedule and capped retention policy would affect a separate proposition-level benchmark. Archive reconstruction, semantic annotation, and hypothetical collector evaluation are different stages; possession of an archive does not establish the performance of a live collector.

The inspected project literature does not justify a field-wide priority claim. In particular, the local novelty-search log uses stronger certification language than the source audit supports, and investigator work not captured by that search remains a concern~\cite{sourceaudit}. We therefore make no ``first'' claim and do not assert that related policy studies do not exist. The delivered contribution is the replay and evaluation artifact, together with a documented feasibility failure. The intended empirical preservation comparison remains incomplete.

\section{Methodology and Implementation}
\subsection{Source, population, and reconstruction}
The source registry identifies the core export generated on September 3, 2026, retrieved on September 12, with a published cut on revision write date. The final audit recomputed the five expanded-file checksums and found all matched the repository pins~\cite{export,verification}. The full bundle has 4,579 page records, 14,591 revisions, and 19,913 events. Our replay population is DSE: 3,908 held pages and 13,403 held revisions. It includes 5,217 deletion events as background, not as 5,217 independent incidents.

Of those deletions, 1,248 events concern 1,246 titles without a published revision. The implementation must not infer their former contents. Four standalone records named \repo{revert} represent successful edits without held bodies in this model, not restoration of a guessed earlier revision. These populations were recounted from the local export, rather than reported as newly observed agent behavior~\cite{verification,sourceaudit}.

The modeled horizon is May 24 to July 15, 2026, in UTC. Saves replace the current body; successful deletion yields a deleted state; a supported bodyless edit yields a live-but-body-unknown state. Prehistory is unknown. An archival timestamp is not substituted for a save time or assumed to end live availability. Selected event times drive the nominal replay. Unresolved co-timed mutations retain alternative states rather than being ordered by arbitrary IDs. The closed timing-uncertainty convention is a model sensitivity, not a calibrated estimate of historical clock error~\cite{contract}.

\subsection{Evidence units and annotation}
An evidence unit is one scoped proposition supported by exact source material. Posting a proposal, reporting that it succeeded, and independently establishing success are distinct claims. Criticality identifies operational-priority content such as coordination, answer transmission, restriction-workaround proposals or reports, and communication persistence. It is not a label for independently confirmed harm, agent intent, or a short lifetime.

\subsection{Collection, discovery, and retention}
The continuous collectors begin without a preloaded list of all future titles. Each receives the same content-free hypothetical feed, polled every 60 seconds. The feed exposes title, action, and selected event time, not body text, annotations, hashes, private identity, or future revision history. At the primary setting, publication lag is five seconds and GET response delay is zero. A GET obtains the current modeled body at response completion, not the revision that originally triggered a notification~\cite{code,contract}.

P periodically fetches every discovered title believed live or mixed and retains fresh copies without deduplication. PD uses the same repeated fetch schedule with exact deduplication. PCD fetches only dirty, believed-live or mixed titles at each periodic sweep. Its changes are feed-derived; it is not a blind monitor with weaker discovery than E. E coalesces dirty titles and services them by oldest pending event time, then title. At E30, the token bucket has capacity and initial credit 30, refills continuously at 30 body attempts per hour, and is serviced each minute. Feed requests are additional.

Storage uses FIFO capture packets and canonical UTF-8 bodies. Exact deduplication shares identical bodies with reference counts, not semantic similarity. Packets still charge title and acquisition provenance. The cap is 1,048,576 bytes for the primary store $S$. Feed metadata $M$ is outside that cap and must be reported separately, including synchronized $S+M$ and byte-time integrals. Oversize captures are rejected before eviction. Failed, unknown, rejected, and pending body attempts still count as requests. These are modeled byte and request costs, not dollar costs or deployment-performance measurements.

The code also implements F, a one-shot terminal live directory and GET baseline; PCD-R, online reacquisition when a title's last known body provenance is no longer retained; reverse service ordering; and a hypothetical persistent terminal archive. PCD-R fetches the current body, not a stored old revision. Reverse E changes only title tie-breaking, not oldest-event priority. The archive enumerates all held revisions saved by the endpoint and charges retrieval and metadata, using the existing store. These controls exist in code and tests, but none produced a completed scientific X13 row. F is not guaranteed to be a coverage lower bound, and capped archive recovery is not an optimized ceiling. Uncapped archive-only is the separate recoverable-release reference.

\subsection{Evaluation and prespecified statistics}
The evaluator accepts retained packets, referenced bodies, and charged delivered context. It validates hashes, packet/object consistency, reference counts, capacity, and times. Diagnostic downloads, rejected bodies, and evicted contents are not interchangeable with retained evidence. A core proposition is covered when an entire eligible support alternative is present: AND within an alternative, OR across alternatives. Context-complete coverage additionally requires its context bundle, so context alone cannot satisfy a missing core~\cite{code}.

Critical coverage restricts the eligible denominator to critical propositions. Overall coverage includes eligible noncritical propositions. Core and context-complete coverage use the same scoped denominator. Unknown required context is represented explicitly rather than silently dropped or counted as a known miss. Preservation delay is computed from the earliest complete retained bundle relative to earliest eligible support; unretained units do not have zero delay. The helper APIs support these definitions. The real runner serializes held-out critical core/context and primary stable-mask results, but does not yet export every planned overall, per-unit, delay, or ever-admitted analysis. Schema files alone do not establish those measurements.

Let $e$ be E30's held-out critical core fraction and $c_j$ the corresponding PCD15 fraction at phase $j$. The prespecified comparison is
\begin{equation}
D=100\left(e-\frac{1}{60}\sum_{j=0}^{59}c_j\right),\qquad
\phi_j=\frac{j\Delta}{60},\quad \Delta=15\text{ minutes}.
\end{equation}
The nominal denominator is $K=23$ and the practical threshold is $D\geq10$ percentage points. The request constraint is $R_{E30}\leq\min_jR_{PCD15,j}$ at identical access and cap. Equal allowed capacity does not imply equal realized occupancy or equal total cost. An unmatched E30 cannot be replaced with E100 or E300 to obtain a favorable headline.

The final contract superseded the early clustered-bootstrap proposal. Reporting helpers use exact rational arithmetic for the primary difference, all-phase admissibility, linear phase quantiles, group macro summaries, and leave-one-group-out checks. No bootstrap, p-value, or population confidence interval was produced. The 60 phases are schedules on one benchmark, not 60 independent incidents. Stable-support re-scoring is an exclusion sensitivity on nominal snapshots, not a range over shared counterfactual histories. None of these scientific summaries was computed in the failed run.

\subsection{Measured computational limitation}
The subsequent outcome-blind diagnosis profiled collection only. On the same five-day P15 prefix with allocation tracing, elapsed time decreased from 154.869 to 21.199 seconds, a reported $7.31\times$ speedup. Peak traced allocation decreased from 134,726,566 to 3,255,239 bytes, or $41.39\times$. These are single bounded profiles, not repeated-run means or confidence intervals~\cite{performance}.

The optimization caches immutable state/body/packet work and compacts diagnostic histories. The equivalence verifier compares request schedules, costs, retained packets and bodies, and accounting projections on bounded prefixes and synthetic fixtures. Its recorded zero mismatches support those tested equivalences, not an exhaustive proof over all histories. It does not compute benchmark coverage.

After repair, the full ordinal-1 collection remained unfinished at the approximately one-hour stop, with observed RSS about 612.5 MB. The schedule census gives 6,387,883 body GETs and 6,462,764 total attempts for that row. Across 240 P/PD primary and reverse rows, the census gives 1,551,038,656 attempts. These are schedule/workload counts, not completed policy-cost observations. The early-prefix speedup therefore did not demonstrate full-roster feasibility.

The latest runtime amendment is explicitly \repo{BLOCKED_NOT_FROZEN}. Its candidate has 130 collection rows and 61 stable families, retaining all 60 primary PCD phases, E30, a PCD-R phase-zero diagnostic, and F. It has not run. PCD-R overhead, F's terminal ambiguity-limit failure, and conservative deadline feasibility remain unresolved~\cite{runtime}. This report does not present the candidate's extrapolated runtime as a measurement or silently replace the failed roster with it.

A subsequent audit pass, after this report's audited base, found that the runtime repair above had not actually reached PCD-R: the collector dispatched PCD-R to a separate repair routine that always retained full per-request history, and that routine also rescanned the complete retained-capture list once per previously known page on every sweep, a cost unrelated to the caching change described above. Both were corrected, and the correction was independently checked for exact output equivalence against the unmodified routine on real bounded prefixes, with and without response delay; every compared field, including retained evidence, storage snapshot, and request and admission counts, matched exactly~\cite{validation}. Even with both fixes applied, a full-horizon PCD-R phase-zero collection-only run did not complete within this audit's observation window, consistent with a workload closer in scale to the failed P/PD rows than to plain PCD's much smaller schedule. This does not establish PCD-R's actual runtime and does not authorize a further real execution attempt; it only narrows what remains unmeasured.

\subsection{Validation evidence and its limits}
The stored reconstruction comparison contains 321 rows: 317 matches and four matches allowing ambiguity. This audit recounted that CSV; it did not perform a fresh independent reconstruction. The V07 report discloses that its reference author had previously read the main implementation. Its second isolated reconstruction was performed by a fresh AI process, not an independent human teammate~\cite{validation}. These are useful but qualified validation records.

Independent-readiness reports describe adversarial tests of storage accounting, causal ordering, snapshot integrity, and controls, as well as defects and repairs. Those dated approvals attach to specific earlier candidates, not automatically to the performance-repaired code or runtime draft. This report audit also ran the current test suite: 253 tests and five subtests passed. That regression check is not a fresh independent validation study. The retained-only implementation and tests do not constitute an end-to-end completed experiment or independent reproduction of a headline result. The main threat to the intended empirical conclusion is simpler: no scored row exists.

\section{Discussion and Conclusion}
The direct answer to the research question is that the completed work does not establish how much evidence the compared policies preserve. It establishes an operational definition, implementation, benchmark inventory, and a computational barrier to evaluating the prescribed contrast. An inability to finish the first repeated-fetch baseline says nothing about whether that baseline would preserve more or less evidence. The performance profile concerns simulator implementation costs, not which infrastructure defenders should deploy.

The data and evaluation design expose two distinctions that remain necessary for a later comparison. First, common discovery access is essential: our periodic comparator also receives the hypothetical feed. A comparison against blind polling would answer a different question. Second, retained core text and interpretable context are separate outcomes. Neither a byte count nor a copied quotation guarantees the context-complete target.

No empirical recommendation to replace periodic collection with event-derived retention follows. Repair behavior, FIFO ordering, latency, cumulative copies, and terminal archive access remain alternative explanations for any future measured difference. The next engineering work is to establish feasible execution without changing those semantics and to complete the required outputs. Any later scope freeze and authorization must precede scientific outcome access; results from a new run cannot be attributed to the incomplete attempt reported here.

For incident-response practice, this work provides an auditable way to formulate a preservation test, not a validated production logging requirement. Acquisition and containment are not competing conclusions of this experiment. \textbf{Nothing here recommends delaying urgent containment to preserve evidence.} Our defensible conclusion is a bounded one: the benchmark and implementation exist, the prescribed experiment failed computationally, and the policy-preservation hypothesis remains unanswered.

\section{Limitations and Dual-Use / Ethical Considerations}
\subsection*{Dataset and external validity}
The archive is incomplete. Recoverable held bodies, deletion-only records, unsupported source links, unknown prehistory, and ambiguous timestamps must remain distinct. Missing public content is not evidence that the underlying behavior did not occur. Export head/live descriptors and archival clocks do not establish historical HTTP visibility. The DSE replay concerns one platform and incident family, not all autonomous-agent systems. Publisher event totals, page counts, and our enriched propositions are not representative incident counts.

\subsection*{Model, annotation, and inference}
The transition rules, nominal clocks, common feed, current-body GET semantics, token bucket, FIFO eviction, deduplication and atomic terminal access are explicit modeling choices. Continuous collectors discover titles from delivered events, rather than receiving all known pages initially. The shared event interface is hypothetical and may require infrastructure not present historically. Final-state and archive interfaces provide different access and cannot be compared as if they were identical live sensors.

The benchmark encodes semantic judgments, including criticality, alternative support and required context. Exact hash/span verification checks source grounding, not exhaustive semantic recall, representative sampling, distinct agent identity or successful external actions. There is no measured exact-copy versus broader semantic-equivalence sensitivity. The final freeze narrows the original episode and sampling claims. No full timestamp-trajectory sensitivity, baseline robustness result or empirical phase variance was produced. Even a later completed replay would estimate a conditional policy intervention within its model, not establish causal effects in a live historical deployment. The present run establishes no policy effect at all.

\subsection*{Agency and moral status}
Names in page text and actor labels are not authenticated agent identities. We must not over-attribute coherent intention, consciousness, suffering or moral status from apparent coordination or persistence language. Conversely, operationally relevant behavior should not be dismissed merely because it originated from a model: posted instructions and tool actions can matter for response even when intent is unknown. This project does not investigate or establish model consciousness, sentience or moral status.

\subsection*{Distressing content, safety, and dual use}
The inspected records include incident and circumvention material. This report avoids reproducing payloads or unnecessary incident quotations. The repository's ingestion and replay operate on downloaded public evidence offline; this work did not require unauthorized access, intrusion, execution of embedded URLs, or offensive interaction with the original systems. This is not a claim that the original incident itself was benign.

Preservation analysis could be misused to identify monitoring gaps or tune evasion timing. We publish no measured evasion-optimal schedule here and do not convert the historical records into operational instructions. Raw third-party logs are excluded from Git under the project's data policy, with a pinned download recipe instead. That does not settle rights to all annotation excerpts, prove blanket permission to redistribute the source, or establish that every public artifact has passed disclosure review. No completed external disclosure approval was verified in this audit. Urgent containment must not be delayed for evidence collection on the basis of this work.

\subsection*{AI assistance and author information}
The audit history explicitly records AI-assisted reasoning, coding and verification, including an isolated AI reconstruction. AI assistance was also used to audit and revise this report. These processes must not be described as wholly independent human validation or as unaided team writing. The authors must review and approve the submission text. The author block uses the supplied names and disciplinary descriptions only; no institutional affiliation or email was verified in the repository. Institutional details require author confirmation before submission.
